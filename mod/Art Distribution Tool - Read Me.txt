ART DISTRIBUTION TOOL
=====================

Art Distribution Tool lets you install several art mods at once (paintings, posters, signs, magazines) and
spread their pictures across the Commonwealth by the percentages you choose. Set GPG Painting Replacer
Collection to 40% and Signs Re Texture to 30%: 40% of the paintings and 30% of the signs they replace show
their art, and the rest stay vanilla. Every picture frame gets its own pick.

Works with Fallout 4 versions 1.10.163 (old-gen), 1.10.984 (next-gen), 1.11.169 and 1.11.240 with the
same files. Works with Mod Organizer 2 and Vortex.


REQUIREMENTS
------------
1. F4SE for your game version (f4se.silverlock.org).
2. Mod Configuration Menu (Nexus 21497), any version from 1.39 on.
3. Runtime Database (Nexus 108394). The plugin uses it to run on every game version.


INSTALL (both managers)
-----------------------
1. Install the requirements above.
2. Install "Art Distribution Tool" with your mod manager like any other mod (it is a FOMOD, click through it).
   Make sure ArtDistributionTool.esl is enabled in your plugin list. The converter is inside the mod at
   Tools\ArtDistributionTool\ArtDistributionToolConverter.exe.
3. Install the art mods you want to use with your mod manager, as you would without Art Distribution Tool, and
   pick whatever their installers offer. Art mods are replacers: they change the game's own paintings,
   posters, signs, magazines, props and set dressing, Pip-Boy or main menu. Standalone art mods, which add
   new objects of their own through their own plugin (buildable posters, new magazines), need no converting:
   they keep working next to Art Distribution Tool.
4. Open the converter (see CONVERT below), press "Find installed art mods", untick what you do not want,
   press Convert. The converter reads each mod as you installed it. Afterwards you can disable the original
   art mods you converted.
   A mod that also adds objects of its own through its plugin can stay enabled: where the sliders pick
   Vanilla, Art Distribution Tool shows the game's own files, not the mod's.
5. Mod Organizer 2: press F5, enable the new "Art Distribution Tool Packs" mod and put it BELOW Art Distribution Tool.
   Vortex: open Downloads, install "Art Distribution Tool Packs.zip", enable it, let it override Art Distribution Tool.
6. Start the game. Open MCM, Art Distribution Tool, and set each pack's slider (see MENU below). Each pack has
   one slider, on the page with most of its art; its number is the percent of everything the pack replaces
   that shows its art (packs on the Other replacers page have an on/off switch instead). The game's own art
   fills whatever the packs leave.
   Changes wait until you apply them: press "Apply all" on the main page, or answer "Apply all" when
   you leave Art Distribution Tool or the menu with changes not applied. After you confirm, the game saves as
   ADTApply, returns to the main menu and loads that save, so every change shows at once (one loading
   screen; the ADTApply save is overwritten each time). "Cancel" when leaving puts every setting back
   to how it was.


CONVERT
-------
Start ArtDistributionToolConverter.exe. The quickest way is to add it as a tool in your manager once:
  Mod Organizer 2: the executables list (the gears icon), Add, browse to
    <your instance>\mods\Art Distribution Tool\Tools\ArtDistributionTool\ArtDistributionToolConverter.exe
  Vortex: Dashboard, Add tool, browse to Data\Tools\ArtDistributionTool\ArtDistributionToolConverter.exe in your
    game folder after deployment.
You can also double-click the exe directly.

The window finds your Mod Organizer 2 instances and Vortex by itself:
  Mod Manager        pick the instance (or Vortex). When you start the converter from inside a manager it
                     preselects that manager.
  FO4 Folder         filled from the instance; the game's own art is read from your game archives, not copied.
  Find installed art mods   lists the installed mods of that manager that hold art the converter takes
                     (Mod Organizer 2: enabled and disabled mods; Vortex: its staging folder). Enabled mods
                     are ticked for you; untick what you do not want, and tick any other row to add it.
                     Every run rebuilds all packs from the ticked mods. When a mod you leave unticked is
                     enabled (Mod Organizer 2) and replaces objects that the ticked mods also replace, the
                     log and the finish message name it and the number of objects it shares with them.
  Convert            writes the "Art Distribution Tool Packs" mod (or, for Vortex, a zip in its downloads).
When you open the converter again after a conversion, the list shows the mods your packs were converted
from, marked "converted", and the status line gives the date. Nothing needs converting again unless your
art mods changed.

Each mod becomes one pack, named after the mod in your manager. To give a pack another name, rename the mod
in your manager before you convert. Two packs with the same name show as two sliders with the same label, so
rename one of them. A renamed pack counts as a new pack: its slider starts at 0.
A mod converts as you installed it: when its installer offered a choice, the pack holds what you chose. To
use another choice, reinstall the mod with it and convert again.
Loading screens: the game shows a turning magazine on some of its loading screens. A magazine cover pack can
add its covers to the loading screens the same way, set on the "Loading screens" page; nothing else of a pack
ever goes on a loading screen. If you use ALR (Automated Loading Screen Replacer), Art Distribution Tool leaves the
loading screens to it.
The log in the window and ADT\convert-report.txt in the output list what was found in each mod.
Each category page has 10 pack slots; the report names any pack that got no slider.
A mod whose items are all in another mod you convert, file for file (a cut-down copy of the same mod),
is left out; a warning names the mod that already has them.
A patch joins its mod's pack. When two mods you convert come from the same Nexus page and replace at least
one of the same objects, the smaller one becomes part of the larger one's pack, under the larger one's name
and slider, and its version of the shared objects is used; the log names every patch it joined. The
converter reads the Nexus page from Mod Organizer 2's record of the mod, or, under Vortex, from the page
number in the mod's folder name.

What the converter recognizes. Only art counts: files in the game's set dressing, props and loading screen
art folders, signs, quest pictures, the Pip-Boy, and the main menu. Everything else a mod replaces (bodies,
faces, clothes, armor, weapons, interface, landscape, sky, sounds) stays with that mod; the log lists what
was left, so keep such a mod installed for it. "Find installed art mods" lists only mods with art.
  - Main menu replacers: Video\MainMenuLoop.bk2 (still or animated). Each such mod becomes a choice of the
    "MENU ART" entry in the main menu, between SETTINGS and CREW; each press moves to the next choice.
    A pick restarts the main menu with that video; the choice is kept in ADT\state.json.
  - Pip-Boy replacers: the screen background (textures\AnimObjects\Pipboy\PipBoyScreen_d.dds) and the
    device skin (PipBoy01_d, PipBoy02_d, PipBoyLabels01_d, PipBoyInterior01_d, PipBoyGlow01_d) get their own
    "Pip-Boy" page: one list for the screen background and one for the skin, Vanilla or one pack each.
  - Textures at vanilla art paths (replacer mods): each picture is tied to the vanilla frame it replaces.
  - Any other texture or material over one of the game's own (props, food boxes, bottles, signs): it goes
    on the "Other replacers" page, or on the page of its kind.
  - Models over one of the game's own, and models or names a mod's plugin gives the game's own objects.
    Pip-Boy models follow the Pip-Boy skin list.
  - Textures the game loads by their name rather than through a material: the Pip-Boy screen effects
    (these follow the Pip-Boy screen list), and texture sets of art objects. The Pip-Boy world map, perk
    chart and Pip-Boy interface files are not converted; they stay with their mod.
  - The main menu theme (Music\Special\MUS_MainTheme), which follows the main menu list.
  - Main menu interface files over the game's own.
  - A mod plugin's changes to the game's own material swaps and texture sets.
  - Materials (.bgsm) at vanilla art paths that point at new textures.
  - A adt-pack.json in the mod (optional) listing pictures and categories explicitly.
Each replacement shows only on the objects its mod replaces, just as the mod does on its own. Left alone:
a mod's new objects and their pictures (its own plugin shows them) and textures nothing in the game uses.

Command line (for scripts): ArtDistributionToolConverter --game "<Fallout 4 folder>" --out "<mods folder>\Art Distribution Tool Packs" <installed mod folders...>


MENU
----
  Picture pages    Paintings, Posters, Signage, Magazines and Quest, each shown when a pack's slider sits
                   there. A pack's slider sits on the page with most of its art and sets all of its art:
                   Alternative Magazine Covers at 40 on Magazines shows its art on 40% of its magazines and
                   on 40% of the 4 paintings it also replaces. The slider's help line says what it sets.
                   The sliders are independent: moving one changes no other. Where two packs replace the
                   same object, their numbers split it; under 100 together the rest is the game's own, over
                   100 they are scaled down to 100.
                   Packs whose art on a page is all for Far Harbor or Nuka-World sit under that location's
                   heading, and the rest (packs that replace base-game files) under "Base game".
  Magazine backs   have no slider. A magazine's back shows the version of that back from the pack its
                   cover shows, or the game's own back when that pack has none; a vanilla cover keeps a
                   vanilla back. A pack with only backs gets no slider, so its backs are not used;
                   the converter warns about it.
  Other replacers  packs whose art is mostly props and set dressing: food, bottles, TVs, bins, crates, and
                   their models. One on/off switch per pack: on shows the pack on every object it
                   replaces, off shows the game's own. Packs that are on for the same object share it
                   evenly; the switch's help line gives the split.
  Pip-Boy page     two lists, screen background and skin: Vanilla or the pack the Pip-Boy wears.
  Loading screens page   one slider per pack with magazine covers: the percent of all loading screens that
                   show one of its covers, on the game's own turning magazine with the pack's back. 100
                   makes every loading screen a pack magazine, 0 none; the rest are the game's own.
  Apply all        applies every change on these pages (see step 6 above). Leaving Art Distribution Tool or the
                   menu with changes not applied asks "Apply all" or "Cancel"; Cancel puts every setting back.
  Shuffle layout   picks a new arrangement with the same slider settings. The arrangement otherwise
                   stays the same across saves and loads until you apply a change.
  New layout on every load   off by default; when on, every save you load gets a new arrangement.
  Reset all settings   back to defaults: all art vanilla (every pack slider at 0), Pip-Boy screen and skin
                       on Vanilla, new layout on every load off, layout seed 1,
                       vanilla main menu video.
  When the converter is run again and the packs it writes changed (a mod added or removed, for example),
  every setting goes back to its default by itself, as with Reset all settings, and a message says "the
  art packs changed, settings were reset". Converting the same mods again keeps your settings.


NOTES
-----
  - Pictures inside precombined meshes (almost all placed art) are drawn from data the game builds when
    an area loads, which is why Apply all reloads the save.
  - An item you pick up keeps the look it had in the world in your inventory, and again when you drop it.
  - Uninstall: disable both mods. Items you picked up while they showed a pack's name keep that name,
    like a renamed item; everything else goes back to vanilla.
  - Damaged install: when files of Art Distribution Tool are missing or changed (an install or update that
    did not finish, for example), the game shows a message naming them at startup. Open the converter: under
    Mod Organizer 2 it restores them from the archive the mod was installed from and lists what it restored.
    Where that archive is gone, or under Vortex, reinstall Art Distribution Tool with your mod manager.
  - Log file: Documents\My Games\Fallout4\F4SE\ArtDistributionTool.log.
